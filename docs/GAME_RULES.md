# Game rules

Items marked **(proposed)** are working defaults that the owner has not confirmed. Keep each in a single constant.

## The puzzle

- The board is N x N and is split into exactly N color zones.
- Each zone is contiguous (cells connect by their sides). Zone sizes are free: a zone can be 1 cell or most of the board.
- Goal: place N cats so that there is exactly one cat in every row, every column and every zone.
- Cats cannot touch each other, not even diagonally.
- Every puzzle has exactly one solution.

## Cells and taps

- A cell is empty, marked with an x, or holds a cat.
- Single tap on an empty cell: marks an x.
- Single tap on an x: erases it, back to empty. Decided by the owner.
- Double tap on a cell, or press and hold it: places a cat. Both gestures work, and both are deliberate enough that a cat is never placed by accident. Decided by the owner.
- Single tap on a cat: removes it, back to empty.
- The first tap of a double tap acts at once (the x appears immediately); the second tap, if it lands on the same cell within a short window, turns that x into a cat. No tap ever waits to find out whether a second one is coming.
- Press and hold places the cat after a short hold (about half a second) without moving the finger. Moving the finger before that turns the touch into a drag instead.
- An x is only the player's note. It never costs anything.
- **Drag to mark or erase:** sliding a finger across the board changes many cells in one stroke. The cell where the drag starts decides what it does. Decided by the owner.
  - **Starts on an empty cell: marks.** Every empty cell it passes gets an x. Cells that already have an x are left as they are, so the player can sweep across a row without caring what is already marked, which matters on large boards with small cells.
  - **Starts on an x: erases.** Every x it passes is cleared. Empty cells are left as they are.
  - A drag never places or removes a cat, and never costs a life. Cells that hold a cat are skipped in both modes.
  - Each cell changes at most once per stroke, even if the finger crosses it twice.
  - A short touch that does not leave its starting cell is still a normal tap.
- Placing a cat on a cell that is not part of the solution is a mistake: the cat is rejected, the cell stays marked with an x, and the player loses 1 life and some points.
- Auto-mark is an option, off by default. When on, cells ruled out by a placed cat (same row, column, zone, and neighbors) show an x automatically. It follows the option at once, even when changed in Options during a level: turning it on also marks around the cats already placed, and Try again uses the current setting. Decided by the owner.

## Options

The Options screen lets the player change:

- **Language:** Portuguese or English. The default follows the device language, falling back to Portuguese.
- **Mode:** night (dark) or day (light). The default follows the device.
- **Sound effects:** on or off, on by default.
- **Auto-mark:** on or off, off by default.
- **Backup code:** export and import (see Saving).
- **About:** a screen about the game and its author: who made it, why, and how to get in touch. Written by the owner. Decided by the owner.

Every choice is saved with the player's progress.

## Sound

Short retro sound effects, generated by code in the style of old consoles to match the pixel art. No music in the first version.

| Moment | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Tap a cell to mark an x | Very short, soft click |
| Place a cat | Short rising blip |
| Remove a cat or an x | Short falling blip |
| Mistake (life lost) | Low buzz |
| Level won | Short rising jingle, three or four notes |
| Level lost | Short falling jingle |
| Reveal or Eraser used | Sparkle |
| Purchase in the shop | Coin sound |
| Boss restoration | The celebration song, with the music on (see Music below); with it off, a rising sweep during the silhouette and a chime when the cat appears |

- **Music:** a soft loop plays while a level is being played, and stops on the win or loss screen. Regular levels have a chill loop; Boss levels a slightly tenser one. A won level plays a cute music box song once (with a tiny "mi-au" before its last note); a Boss beaten for the first time plays the celebration song instead. A lost level plays a sad song once, in A minor, with the loss jingle on top; retrying stops it at once and the level music starts again. The Boss restoration has its own celebration song, timed to the animation: a tense build-up while the ghost flies in, turns black and flickers, a fanfare when the cat appears, then a happy loop while the screen stays open. Every screen outside a level (menu, map, shop and Options) shares a funky chill loop that ends with a soft meow, and it keeps playing from one to the next. It has its own switch in Options, separate from the sound effects, and is on by default. Decided by the owner.
- Sound starts only after the player's first tap, because browsers block audio before any interaction.
- Turning sound off in Options silences everything at once and is saved with the player's progress.
- On iPhone, the device's silent switch may also mute the game.

## Lives, score and time

- 3 lives per level. Lives exist only inside a level and reset on every attempt.
- 3 mistakes lose the level. The player can retry immediately, as many times as they want. There is no energy, cooldown or attempt limit.
- Score = 500, minus 50 per mistake, plus a time bonus. Decided by the owner.
- Time bonus: 2 points for every second under the board's par time, and nothing past it. Par time grows with the board: 2 seconds per cell, so 50 s on 5x5 and 288 s on 12x12. Accepted by the owner as the starting point; tune after playtesting.
- The score never goes below zero.
- A timer runs per level. The best time is kept per board size.

## Helps

Bought with coins at the moment of use. No per-level counters.

| Help | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal | Places one correct cat | 50 coins **(proposed)** |
| Eraser | Removes every x that sits on a solution cell; free if there is none | 20 coins **(proposed)** |
| Lamp | Points out the next logical step without placing the cat | later, needs the human-style solver |

## Currencies

- Two currencies: coins and fish. Both are earned only by playing levels.
- Nothing costs real money and there are no ads.
- Coins buy helps. Fish buy shop cats.
- Reward for clearing a level: 40 coins and 2 fish **(proposed)**. Boss levels give a larger prize **(proposed)**.

## Cats

- The cat sprite on the board is the player's selected cat. Cat heads blink.
- The menu shows the player's selected cat in full body, sitting and facing the player, blinking. Decided by the owner.
- Cats have three body types: slim, regular and chubby. Decided by the owner.
- World cats are generated (body type plus colors, fixed per world). Shop cats are the special ones, hand-drawn by the owner.
- The white cat is owned from the start.
- Each world has its own cat to restore. The cat starts as a little ghost; beating the world's Boss turns the ghost back into a cat and adds it to the player's collection.
- The ghost and the world's scenery appear only on the Boss level, since each world has exactly one cat. Regular levels show neither.
- During the Boss, the ghost floats at the top right of the screen, above the board, waiting for the player to finish.
- Beating the Boss plays the restoration: the screen switches to a plain neutral background, the ghost moves to the center, turns into a black silhouette, the silhouette flickers into the shape of the cat, and the cat is revealed in full color, full body, with its name. The cat head is only the icon used on the board and menus; the reveal shows the whole cat. The neutral background is there so nothing competes with the cat.
- Other cats are exclusive to the shop and cost fish.
- Each world is shown as a small board-style map: a path of tiles, one per level, ending at the Boss, with the world's cat waiting at the end as a silhouette until it is rescued.
- The player's selected cat stands on the map tile of the current level. After a level is cleared, the cat walks along the path to the next tile, in the style of the world maps of classic platform games. Decided by the owner.
- Tapping an unlocked tile makes the cat walk there. Tapping the same tile again opens its level once the cat stands on it, and the Play button opens the level of the tile the cat is on. Locked tiles do nothing. Decided by the owner.
- **Biomes (proposed by the owner, details to be defined):** each world has a theme that sets the look of its map: ground and path colors, decorations (trees, palm trees, a river with a bridge, tombstones...) and the layout of the path.
- Each world also has a time of day: night, day or afternoon (pink sky). The biome and the time are picked together at random, seeded by the world number, so a world always looks the same; two worlds in a row never get the same pair. World 1 is the meadow at night. The map follows the world's time of day, not the player's night or day mode. Decided by the owner (`src/game/biomes.ts`).
- Each world's path also has its own shape: the plain snake (4 tiles per row), a wide snake (3 per row) or a staircase, each one flipped left to right or not. World 1 is the plain snake; every later world picks a shape different from the world before it, at random but always the same for the same world, so the path from level to level is not the same in every world. The Boss is always alone at the top, centered. Decided by the owner (`worldRoute` in `src/game/biomes.ts`, drawn by `pathLayout.ts`).
- For now the biomes are meadow and beach, with the mockup's placeholder decorations: ground specks, bushes on the meadow, fireflies at night, the sea along the beach's right edge. They are placed at random, seeded by the world number. More biomes, times and drawn decorations are added as the owner draws them.
- A biome ties the world together: its map, its Boss scenery and, ideally, the cat restored there share the same theme (a graveyard world restoring a black cat, a beach world restoring an orange one) **(proposed)**.
- Starting biomes **(proposed)**: meadow, forest, beach, river, graveyard, snow. The graveyard stays cute, not scary, in both modes.
- Every biome has a palette for each time of day.
- Decorations never sit on the path or touch a level tile, so the tiles and the cat always read clearly.
- Starting set **(names proposed)**: Mingau (white, default), Caramelo (orange), Fumaça (gray), Meia-noite (black).

## Shop

- **Daily rotation:** each day the shop offers 4 cats, picked at random from the shop pool. Decided by the owner.
- **The shop pool is the shop-exclusive cats only.** World cats, restored by beating a world's Boss, never appear in the shop: they are earned by exploring. Decided by the owner.
  - Example: Meia-noite, the black cat, is restored in exploration, so it can never be in the shop. The same holds for every world cat. Decided by the owner.
  - Until the owner draws shop-exclusive cats, the pool is empty and the shop shows only helps.
- **Rotation cats are limited:** a shop cat can be bought only on a day it is in the rotation. A cat missed today comes back on a later day. Decided by the owner.
- Cats the player already owns stay in the rotation, marked sold out, so every player sees the same 4 cats on the same day. A day can therefore offer fewer than 4 cats still to buy. When the pool has fewer than 4 cats, the shop shows all of them. Decided by the owner.
- Buying a cat frees its slot: the next cat in the day's draw takes it, so the shop keeps showing up to 4 cats the player can still buy **(proposed)**.
- The shop also lists the player's own cats, so they can choose which one plays **(proposed)**.
- The rotation changes at midnight on the device's clock. It is picked from the date alone, with no server, so every player sees the same 4 cats on the same date **(proposed)**.
- No countdown to the next rotation is shown, so the shop never works as a wait timer **(proposed)**.
- Helps, priced in coins, are always on sale and are not part of the rotation.

## Progression

- Levels are numbered from 1 across all worlds. The level number is the seed of its puzzle, so a level is always the same board.
- Levels are grouped into worlds. Each world starts on a small board, grows level by level and ends in a Boss, so difficulty rises and resets in peaks instead of climbing forever.
- Worlds come in three tiers, and the player moves through them in order:

| Tier | Levels per world, Boss included | Board growth inside the world | Starting cat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 5 | Fast, short maps | Given on some levels |
| Intermediate | 8 | Slower | None |
| Advanced | 10 to 15 | Slowest | None |

- A starting cat is one correct cat already placed when the level opens. It is the teaching aid of the early worlds.
- A starting cat is fixed: tapping, holding or dragging over it never removes it, and it is placed again on a retry. It is always the same cell for the same level **(proposed)**.
- Every world opens with a breather: its first levels are clearly easier than the Boss the player has just beaten, so the game never feels like one long climb. This holds in every tier, the advanced one included.
- Early worlds step all the way back to 5x5. From the intermediate tier on, a world steps back only a few sizes below the previous Boss, so the breather does not feel like a return to the tutorial **(proposed)**.
- The largest board is never introduced all at once: each tier raises the ceiling a little.
- World 1, as defined by the owner: level 1 is 5x5 with a starting cat, level 2 is 5x5 without one, the next levels grow, and the Boss is 9x9 with a starting cat.
- World 2: starts at 5x5 with no starting cat, grows with no help, and is capped at 9x9.
- The largest board in the game is 12x12, and only the hardest worlds reach it. Decided by the owner.
- Once the game has reached its largest board (12x12), later worlds grow in two ways instead of through bigger boards:
  - **Longer worlds:** each world has gradually more levels than the one before, so the journey to the Boss keeps getting longer. Decided by the owner.
  - **Harder boards:** more evenly sized zones and fewer single-cell zones.
- The growth is gradual: one more level every world or two, starting from the length of the first advanced worlds, up to a cap so a single world never becomes a grind. Starting length 12, one more level every two worlds, cap at 20 levels **(proposed)**.

Worlds table **(proposed)**. Rows 1 and 2 follow the owner's definition; the sizes in between and rows 3 onward are a first draft. Worlds past the end of the table are produced by a rule (length from the world number, sizes from the advanced pattern), so the game never runs out of worlds.

## Replaying

- The player can go back to any world already cleared by **swiping the map sideways**: swipe right for the previous world, left for the next. Decided by the owner, to keep the screen clean (no row of world buttons).
- Small arrow buttons beside the world title do the same, for accessibility and for players who do not discover the swipe. Dots under the title show which world is on screen.
- Swiping onto a locked world shows its map dimmed and marked as locked, with what unlocks it.
- In a cleared world every tile is open and the cat walks to any of them.
- A beaten Boss shows no ghost: the restored cat sits beside the Boss tile in full body. Replaying the Boss is only for the challenge and the record.
- Each tile shows the player's best score for that level.
- **Rewards on replay only when the player beats their previous best score on that level.** Decided by the owner. Beating it gives coins only, no fish, so fish stay a reward for moving forward **(proposed)**.
- For there to be something to beat after a perfect run, the score includes a time bonus: faster clears score higher. Decided by the owner; it also gives the timer a purpose.

## Screen conventions

- The Boss level of each world has a pixel-art scenery strip above the board. There are several scenery drawings and the Bosses rotate through them; a given world always shows the same one. The drawing is shared; each world recolors it with its own palette. Scenery, ghost and everything else on screen is pixel art only.

- A strip of cat silhouettes above the board fills in as cats are placed.
- The three rules live behind a "!" button, not as permanent text.
- Cells are separate tiles with a gap between them; zones are told apart by color.
- No black outlines anywhere.
- Zone colors alone stop being distinguishable somewhere past 8 zones. Larger boards need a texture or pattern per zone in addition to color.

## Saving

- Progress is stored on the device. It lasts until the site's data is cleared.
- A level in progress is never saved. Closing the app or leaving the level discards its marks, cats, lives, score and time, and the level starts from scratch next time. This is a rule of the game, not a missing feature.
- The player can export a backup code (text) and import it later or on another device.
